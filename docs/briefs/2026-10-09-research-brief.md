# Research Paper — engagement brief

## The problem
As a junior congressperson from Hawaii, newly assigned to the House Judiciary Subcommittee on the Administrative State, Regulatory Reform, and Antitrust, you need to deliver policy goals and recommendations to the Office of Legislative Counsel to be included in the initial draft of a new bill that will be introduced on the House floor later this term. The topic you have chosen is that of Aggregators, internet-based companies that seem to exert extreme levels of market dominance in some cases (e.g. Google, Facebook), but that current law seems inadequate to address. Company breakups, for example, have proven ineffective, because another aggregator can come along afterward to fill the gap.

Legislation will not address the basic principles that make aggregators work (near-zero marginal cost, strong network effects, user preference for the superior service, etc.) or any horizontal M&A deals that have already taken place (e
.g. Facebook acquiring Instagram). What it can do is create levers that regulators can use to prevent market dominance occurring in the future.

You need to make sure your bill has real substance without being too ambitious, because bills that try to do too much generally die in committee, and bills that are watered down can add compliance cost without meaningfully changing outcomes. At the same time, you need to ensure your definitions and reasoning are sound, because statutes are much harder to change than agency rules (i.e. regulations) that are written by administrative agencies. Lastly, you don’t want to risk your legislation leading to over-regulation of businesses that create consumer surplus. Not only would this be unpopular, it would also destroy real value.

## What I am assuming
Unlike traditional monopolies that control supply and distribution, aggregators derive power from controlling demand. Marginal cost of serving each additional user is effectively zero, and greater scale creates greater value. A virtuous cycle results from good user experience that attracts more users, which attracts more suppliers, which then creates a better user experience. Consumer choice, then, is truly genuine and not a matter of high switching costs. While it is true that alternatives are always “only a click away,” users have very little incentive to switch. With an aggregator, market equilibrium is monopoly; one aggregator that has captured all of the consumers and all of the suppliers.
