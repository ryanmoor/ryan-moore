# Perfect Competition — recommendation

**Recommendation.** Plant 10 beds of tomatoes, 20 beds of carrots, and 30 beds of mesclun. Every bed in this plan would earn more than it costs to grow.

## Why
Given the information we have, it’s best to stop at 10 beds of tomatoes, just before marginal cost ($8,249) exceeds price ($8,800). An 11th bed of tomatoes would cost $9,391 and profitability would start to decline. Carrots and mesclun can each be planted to their bed caps with price still above marginal cost.

## The judgment call
You could exceed the bed caps and earn an additional $1,138 profit. Three additional beds of carrots would earn $352, $298 and $242, and one additional bed of mesclun would earn $246. Therefore the most it’s worth paying for the 21st carrot bed is $352. This model does not know whether the caps can be adjusted, so you should plant 10/20/30 and treat raising the caps as a separate decision.

## What would change my answer
If the price of tomatoes rises by 6.7% ($591) or more, you should plant an 11th bed. If it falls by 6.3% or more, you should drop to nine beds.
